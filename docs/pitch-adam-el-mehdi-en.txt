What motivates me is the moment a difficult problem starts to make sense.

I’m Adam El Mehdi, a first-year engineering student in Fluid Mechanics, Energy and Environment at ENSEEIHT, in Toulouse.

In teams, I often take the lead or check the reasoning behind a decision. I like finding ways to improve things, and classmates sometimes ask me to explain difficult topics.

After two years of preparatory classes in Rabat, moving to France meant rebuilding my daily life. It hasn’t always been easy, but I’m learning to find my feet in a new environment and build my own path.

My TIPE was another step outside my comfort zone: my first time running a physics experiment on my own. I built a magnetic braking setup, used Arduino for measurements and Python for analysis. Under the conditions studied, a slit copper tube had a braking coefficient about twenty percent lower than an intact tube. I had to connect theory with measurements I had collected myself.

During the IMSA Sentinels hackathon, I contributed to planning, prompts and simple programming within a team.

Aircraft have fascinated me since childhood. Now, I’m exploring what working in aeronautics could mean.

Pascal, a retired Airbus engineer, highlighted leadership and the working environment. Gerrard, a retired ONERA researcher, stressed adaptability and earning trust. His “two thousand Mondays” idea reflects what I want: meaningful progress and a team whose company I enjoy.

Laurent’s work in Airbus aircraft certification also interests me because it connects simulation, flight testing and safety through evidence.

My next steps are to strengthen my skills in fluid mechanics and numerical methods, and explore simulation, testing and validation. For a study period abroad, I’m considering TU Clausthal, TU Hamburg-Harburg and TU Berlin. I want to deepen my technical knowledge while adapting to another culture.

Outside engineering, I enjoy philosophy, football and bodybuilding. They reflect my curiosity and my desire to keep challenging myself.

I want to bring that mindset to simulation or testing in aerospace or energy: solve difficult problems, keep learning and help a team move forward.
